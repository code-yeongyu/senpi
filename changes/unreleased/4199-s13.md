## 2026-10-27 - Fence, retire and tombstone abandoned reservation generations (desktop#4199)

### What changed

- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/endpoint-reservation-store.ts`: moves to schema 5. `retire` retires a fenced (`cancelling`) generation at the revision its census was taken at (`revision_changed` otherwise), only with a proof (`presence_released` or `process_gone`) for exactly the reserving owner until its `released` commit and every granted role without one (`discharge_incomplete` otherwise), and persists the discharges with the fenced revision. A retired generation refuses every later claim, offer, read of an offer, grant, serving commit, fence and retirement with `generation_retired`. `reserve` refuses a second generation for an endpoint while an earlier one is not retired (`generation_active`). `activeGenerations` and `roleRows` list what recovery reads.

### Why

- Design 4.2a, "Crash, cancellation and commit" and "Recovery transaction order" steps 2, 4 and 5: cancellation is a durable fence, retirement needs every recorded authority entry discharged and the fenced revision revalidated, the census result is persisted with the fenced generation, tombstones keep delayed actors from reviving a retired generation, and exactly one successor is admitted.

### Why an extension could not handle it

- The reservation store runs before extensions load and owns the endpoint's launch admission.

### Expected merge conflict zones

- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/endpoint-reservation-store.ts`: schema SQL, the rejection union, `reserve`, `cancel` and the row mapping.

## 2026-10-27 - The recovery transaction: probe set, admissibility, kernel-session gate and census (desktop#4199)

### What changed

- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/endpoint-recovery.ts` (new): `recoverEndpointReservations` recovers every generation of an endpoint that is not retired, oldest first, under the caller's endpoint lock. It fences the generation, then probes the presence objects of the probe set (the reserving owner until its `released`, every granted role without `released`) with one nonblocking attempt each, in presence-ID order and outside every state transaction. The bridge keeps an acquisition only when admissibility rules 1-3 hold. A busy object refuses (`presence_occupied`) whatever other evidence says. An entry without an admissible acquisition is discharged only by exact process evidence (`process_gone`), which counts only on a store recorded and still classified `local` (clause iii) or under verified, equal kernel sessions (clauses i and iv); otherwise it is `authority_live` or `authority_unknown`. It then rereads the generation, drops what committed `released` meanwhile, takes the census of every recorded supervisor, host child and session claim (`census_live`, `census_unknown`), and retires at the revalidated revision while holding the recovered guards, releasing them only after the commit. A creation FILETIME is compared exactly. A changed verified kernel session on a local store whose probed objects were all acquired is the prior-boot proof. Nothing is mutated, signalled or deleted on a refusal; the fence stays. `recoveryProber` reads the prober's kernel session and the store directory's current classification once.
- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/presence-placement.ts`: exports `kernelSession`, the bridge-to-record mapping recovery reuses.

### Why

- Design 4.2a "Recovery transaction order", Revision 7 "Probe set" and "Admissibility", Revision 8 "Process evidence and the kernel-session gate", "Boot provenance and prior-boot recovery"; tests 11, 17 (Windows half), 19, 22, 23 and 29.

### Why an extension could not handle it

- Recovery runs under the endpoint lock before any credential mutation or launch, before extensions load.

### Expected merge conflict zones

- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/presence-placement.ts`: the `kernelSession` export.

## 2026-10-27 - Recover before exactly one successor at startHost (desktop#4199)

### What changed

- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-ensure-start.ts`: a Windows public-endpoint start runs `recoverEndpointReservations` over the endpoint's generations that are not retired, under the endpoint lock, before it acquires the reserving owner's presence, mutates the credential or reserves. A refusal throws `HostEnsureRefusedError` reason `reservation_recovery_required` with `recovery { reason, generation }`.
- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-decision.ts`: `HostRefusalReason` gains `reservation_recovery_required` with its refusal detail; `HostEnsureRefusedError` carries `recovery` (`ReservationRecoveryRefusal`).
- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-runner.ts` and `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-session-runner.ts`: the refusal payload carries `recovery`.
- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/endpoint-recovery.ts`: its refusal type is the shared `ReservationRecoveryRefusal` reason.

### Why

- Design 4.2a "Recovery transaction order" step 5 ("persist retirement, then admit exactly one successor under endpoint exclusion") and "Crash, cancellation and commit" (no successor may publish or start until all claimed and granted authority is proven gone); test 11 at the CLI entry. With the one-successor guard alone, every restart of a Windows public endpoint was refused with `generation_active`.

### Why an extension could not handle it

- Admission runs inside `ensureHost` under the endpoint lock, before extensions load.

### Expected merge conflict zones

- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-ensure-start.ts`: the start prologue before `acquireReservingOwner`.
- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-decision.ts`: `HostRefusalReason`, `HostEnsureRefusedError` and `refusalDetail`.
- `packages/coding-agent/src/modes/rpc/host-runner.ts` and `host-session-runner.ts`: the refusal payloads.

### Changed

- The Windows public-endpoint reservation store keeps a retired generation as a tombstone and admits a new generation only after every earlier one is retired (sisyphuslabs/omo-desktop-app#4199, design 4.2a "Recovery transaction order").
- A Windows public-endpoint start first recovers an earlier startup reservation: it retires it once every process that reservation authorized is proven ended, and otherwise refuses with `reservation_recovery_required` and a typed `recovery { reason, generation }`, without touching the credential or starting anything (sisyphuslabs/omo-desktop-app#4199, test 11).
